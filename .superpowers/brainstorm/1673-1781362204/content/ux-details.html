UX Details — Polishing the ritual

A few remaining decisions to complete the experience design.

Q1: Include 筊杯 (Moon Block) confirmation?

In real temples, after a stick falls, you throw two crescent-shaped wooden blocks (筊杯/杯筊). One flat side up + one round side up = 圣杯 (the deity accepts). Both flat = 笑杯 (deity laughs, try again). Both round = 怒杯 (deity is angry, reconsider your question).

A

Include 筊杯 — Full ritual

Shake → stick falls → throw moon blocks → if 圣杯, reveal fortune. If not, shake again. Adds authenticity and drama.

B

Skip 筊杯 — Streamlined

Shake → stick falls → reveal fortune directly. Simpler flow, less friction for casual users.

Q2: Fortune history & sharing?

1

Save history (localStorage)

Users can see their past fortunes. Stored in browser. Each entry shows: date, deity, tier, stick number, poem snippet.

2

Share card (image/text)

Generate a shareable card with the fortune poem, deity name, and tier. Copy text or download as image. Good for social media virality.

3

Daily limit (1 draw/day)

One free draw per day. Makes each fortune feel special. Prevents spam-clicking. "The celestial court only answers once a day."
