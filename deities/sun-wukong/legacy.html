Across the World

From Peking Opera to Black Myth: Wukong, Dragon Ball to Hollywood — discover how the Monkey King inspired the world.

Quick Answer

**Sun Wukong** achieved Buddhahood as the Victorious Fighting Buddha (斗战胜佛) at the end of Journey to the West, transcending his rebel origins. His cultural legacy spans centuries — inspiring Dragon Ball's Son Goku, Marvel's Monkey King, video games like Black Myth: Wukong, and remaining the most globally recognized figure in Chinese mythology.

Peking Opera
Since the 19th Century

The Monkey King on Stage

Peking Opera transformed Wukong into one of its most iconic roles. With painted face, pheasant-feather headdress, and acrobatic fight choreography, the Monkey King became a staple of Chinese stagecraft. The role demands an actor who can sing, act, and perform breathtaking martial stunts — often all at once. Even today, a great Wukong performance can fill an opera house, and the character's signature "jingju" mask is recognized worldwide.

Television
1986 — present

Journey to the West (1986)

The CCTV television adaptation remains the definitive screen portrayal of Wukong in China. Starring Liu Xiao Ling Tong — a performer from a family lineage devoted to the Monkey King role for generations — the series aired during Chinese New Year and became an instant national treasure. It has been rebroadcast more than 3,000 times, watched by billions. For many Chinese people, the actor's face is the Monkey King. Multiple remakes and film adaptations have followed, but none have eclipsed the 1986 classic.

Manga & Anime
1984 — present

Dragon Ball: The Son Goku Phenomenon

Akira Toriyama openly drew from Journey to the West for the original Dragon Ball. Son Goku — the Japanese reading of "Sun Wukong" — shares the Monkey King's name, his extending pole weapon (the Power Pole / Nyoibo), his flying cloud (the Flying Nimbus / Kinto'un), and his tailed, monkey-like appearance. What began as a loose adaptation became one of the most commercially successful manga and anime franchises in history, introducing the Monkey King archetype to hundreds of millions who had never read the original novel.

Video Games
2024

Black Myth: Wukong

Game Science's Black Myth: Wukong broke global records on release, becoming one of the fastest-selling games in history and earning universal acclaim for its technical artistry. Built on Unreal Engine 5, it reimagines the Journey to the West as a dark fantasy action-RPG, with photorealistic environments drawn from real Chinese temples and landscapes. More than a commercial hit, it marked a watershed moment: a Chinese studio, telling a Chinese story, dominating the Western-dominated AAA game market. The world finally saw Wukong as China has always seen him — epic, complex, and unstoppable.

Film & Animation
1960s — present

Global Cinema

From the 1960s Chinese animated classic Havoc in Heaven — whose hand-drawn artistry influenced generations of animators — to Stephen Chow's irreverent Journey to the West films, to Hong Kong action spectacles starring Donnie Yen and Aaron Kwok, Wukong has been cinema's favorite Chinese myth for decades. Hollywood has repeatedly attempted adaptations, from the animated The Monkey King to Steven Spielberg's long-rumored take. In 2023, Netflix's The Monkey King brought the story to global streaming audiences. Every era reimagines him. None can contain him.

Literature
1592 — present

One Novel, Endless Retellings

Wu Cheng'en's 16th-century novel is one of the Four Great Classical Novels of Chinese literature. Translated into dozens of languages, it has inspired poets and novelists across cultures. Arthur Waley's 1942 English abridgment, titled Monkey, became a Penguin Classics staple. Gene Luen Yang's graphic novel American Born Chinese weaves the Monkey King into the Asian-American experience — and won major literary awards. The original text remains startlingly modern: a satire of bureaucracy, a meditation on enlightenment, and an adventure story that has never gone out of print.
