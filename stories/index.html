Chinese Mythology Stories & Legends

From the birth of the cosmos to the epic Journey to the West — the stories that shaped Chinese civilization

Chinese mythology is built on stories — grand, sweeping narratives that explain the origin of the world, the nature of divinity, and the meaning of heroism. Unlike the fixed canon of Western mythology, Chinese myths evolved over thousands of years through Taoist, Buddhist, and folk traditions, creating a living story cycle that continues to inspire modern film, literature, and art. At the foundation lie creation myths: Pangu Creating the World and Nüwa Repairs the Sky. At the heart sits the epic Journey to the West, the great Chinese novel that brought together gods, demons, monks, and immortals in a single quest for truth. Between them lie legendary battles like Sun Wukong's Havoc in Heaven and divine origin tales such as Nezha's Lotus Rebirth. These are the Chinese myths and Chinese legends that define the tradition — each one a doorway into a rich and wondrous world.

Creation Myths

The beginning of all things — how the cosmos, humanity, and the natural world came into being

Pangu Creates the World From a cosmic egg in the primordial void, Pangu was born. For 18,000 years he grew, pushing heaven and earth apart with his immense body. When he died, his eyes became the sun and moon, his blood the rivers, his breath the wind — his entire being transformed into the world we know. This is the oldest and most foundational of all Chinese creation myths.

Nüwa Repairs the Sky When the sky collapsed and fire rained upon the earth, the goddess Nüwa melted five-colored stones to patch the heavens. She cut off the legs of a giant turtle to prop up the four corners of the world, and the cosmos was restored. This myth explains both the origin of the stars and the Chinese reverence for colored jade.

Nüwa Creates Humanity In the beginning, the earth was beautiful but empty. Nüwa took yellow clay and molded human figures, breathing life into them. When she grew tired, she dipped a rope in mud and flicked it — each droplet becoming another human. The handcrafted ones became nobles; the rope-droplets became commoners. This is the Chinese origin story of humanity.

Iconic episodes from China's greatest mythological novel — a pilgrimage filled with battles, demons, and divine intervention

Havoc in Heaven Sun Wukong, having mastered the 72 Earthly Transformations and acquired the Ruyi Jingu Bang, rebels against the celestial court. He defeats the Four Heavenly Kings, battles Nezha and Erlang Shen, and declares himself "Great Sage, Equal of Heaven." It takes the combined armies of heaven, the cunning of Erlang Shen, and finally the Buddha himself to subdue him.

The Buddha's Palm Sun Wukong, believing he has reached the edge of the universe, finds five pillars and marks them. When he returns, he discovers the pillars were the Buddha's fingers — and the mark he left is on the Buddha's own hand. The Buddha then turns his hand into a mountain of five elements, trapping the Monkey King for 500 years. It is the ultimate lesson in humility.

Erlang Shen, heaven's most powerful warrior with his third eye and faithful hound, meets Sun Wukong in a battle of 72 transformations. Divine Origins

How the gods became gods — origin stories of ascension, cultivation, and divine birth

The Jade Emperor's Cultivation Before he sat on the Dragon Throne, the Jade Emperor was a mortal prince who devoted himself to cultivation and charity. Through countless kalpas of selfless deeds over hundreds of millions of years, he earned his place as the supreme sovereign of heaven. His story embodies the Confucian ideal that virtue and perseverance lead to the highest station.

Nezha's Lotus Rebirth After Nezha's dramatic self-destruction, his soul wanders without a body. His teacher, the immortal Taiyi Zhenren, fashions him a new body from the sacred lotus of the Jade Pool. Nezha emerges reborn — more powerful than before, with the Wind Fire Wheels, the Cosmic Ring, and the Fire-tipped Spear. This rebirth transforms him from a troubled child into a warrior god.

Guanyin's Path to Mercy Guanyin, once a devoted prince named Avalokiteshvara in Buddhist tradition, attained enlightenment but chose to remain in the mortal world rather than enter nirvana. In Chinese folk tradition, she is also venerated as Princess Miaoshan, who endured immense suffering with unwavering compassion. Her thousand arms and thousand eyes symbolize her ability to see and rescue all who suffer.

Frequently Asked Questions About Chinese Myths & Stories

Q: What is the most famous Chinese myth?

The most famous Chinese myth is undoubtedly the Journey to the West, a 16th-century novel by Wu Cheng'en that blends history, mythology, and folklore into an epic adventure. It tells the story of the monk Tang Sanzang's pilgrimage to India with his three disciples — Sun Wukong, Zhu Bajie, and Sha Wujing — to retrieve Buddhist scriptures. The novel is so deeply embedded in Chinese culture that its characters are recognized by virtually every Chinese person, and it has inspired countless films, TV series, and adaptations worldwide.

Q: Is Journey to the West a real story?

Journey to the West is a fictional novel inspired by a real historical event. The monk Xuanzang (602-664 CE) did travel from China to India and back to retrieve Buddhist scriptures — a 17-year journey of over 10,000 miles. However, the novel's supernatural elements — the Monkey King, the demon battles, the heavenly bureaucracy — are fictional additions. The real Xuanzang traveled alone; the fictional Tang Sanzang travels with three supernatural disciples. The blending of real history with Chinese myths is what gives the story its enduring power.

Q: What Chinese myths are good for beginners?

For newcomers to Chinese mythology, the best starting points are the foundational creation myths. Pangu Creates the World is the most accessible origin story — short, vivid, and cosmically grand. Nüwa Repairs the Sky and Nüwa Creates Humanity are equally approachable. For character-driven stories, start with Who Is Nezha? — a tale of rebellion, sacrifice, and rebirth that reads like a modern superhero origin. For an epic adventure, dive into Sun Wukong's Havoc in Heaven and work your way through the Journey to the West cycle.
